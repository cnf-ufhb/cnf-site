Promoting and preserving the plant diversity of Côte d'Ivoire

The Centre National de Floristique (National Floristic Centre) leads research on, and the conservation and sustainable use of, Côte d'Ivoire's botanical heritage through its National Herbarium and Botanical Garden.

Our mission

An institute devoted to research and publication

Established on 11 July 1973 (Decree No. 73-347), the Centre National de Floristique (CNF) is an institute of Félix Houphouët-Boigny University, attached to the Faculty of Biosciences (UFR Biosciences), and dedicated to the study and sustainable use of Côte d'Ivoire's plant diversity. Located in Cocody within a 10.35-hectare urban forest that serves as a botanical garden, it conducts and publishes research, ensures the in situ and ex situ conservation of the Ivorian flora, trains professionals and students, and welcomes the public — schoolchildren, researchers, the private and public sectors, civil society organisations and hikers — to showcase the country's rich flora and its ecosystem services.

“To make known and preserve the botanical heritage of Côte d'Ivoire, for the benefit of science and of the population.”

What we do

Our activities

Seven core missions shape the work of the CNF, from fundamental research to public outreach.

🔬

Research & publication

Scientific research on the Ivorian flora and dissemination of findings to the academic community.

🌿

National Herbarium

Curation and documentation of Côte d'Ivoire's reference botanical collections, founded by Professor Laurent Aké Assi.

🏞️

Botanical Garden & Arboretum

A living space for ex situ conservation, observation and public visits, at the heart of a 10.35-hectare urban forest.

🌳

In situ & ex situ conservation

Action to safeguard Ivorian plant species, including endemic species.

🎓

Certified training

Capacity-building workshops and courses, including the new Professional Skills Certificate in Biodiversity, Conservation and Sustainable Use of Tropical Natural Resources.

🍄

Edible mushrooms

Training sessions on fungal biodiversity and mushroom cultivation, and promotion of wild edible mushrooms.

🚶

School visits & nature walks

Educational visits for primary and lower-secondary school pupils, and discovery walks for hikers in the Centre's forest.

Professor Tiébré Marie-Solange (married name Rensonnet)

Full Professor of Botany (CAMES) — Faculty of Biosciences, Félix Houphouët-Boigny University
PhD in Agricultural Sciences and Biological Engineering (2007) — University of Liège, Gembloux campus, Belgium

  • Ecological restoration
  • Nature-based solutions
  • Invasive alien species
  • Wild edible mushrooms

A Full Professor of Botany (CAMES) since 2019, Marie-Solange Tiébré has been Director of the Centre National de Floristique at Félix Houphouët-Boigny University since December 2023. She oversees the curation and management of the National Herbarium and the Arboretum, as well as the restoration of degraded sites. From 2020 to 2026, she headed the Laboratory of Systematics, Herbaria and Botanical Museum (LSHMB) of the Faculty of Biosciences.

Her fundamental and applied research addresses biodiversity and ecosystem services in the context of climate change, invasive alien species and wild edible mushrooms. She develops innovative approaches to ecological restoration — nature-based solutions, assisted rehabilitation of mining sites, ecotourism — and serves as an expert in environmental and social impact assessment.

She has coordinated ten research and development projects with national and international partners (GIZ, IRD, GBIF, FONSTI, PASRES, the International Foundation for Science, the Royal Belgian Institute of Natural Sciences, and mining and agro-industrial companies), restored four biodiversity refuge forests and led more than 200 reforestation operations. A member in 2019 of the Convention on Biological Diversity's technical expert group on invasive alien species, she has managed Côte d'Ivoire's Biodiversity Clearing-House Mechanism since 2010.
